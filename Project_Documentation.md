## 1. Summary

Project Nokhottro is a 7-minute animated cartoon episode that introduces school-age space enthusiasts to the real hardware NASA has left on the Moon, on Mars and in deep space, and the science it made possible. Kids follow **Spark**, a young spacecraft, as **Moony** (the Moon) and **Marsy** (Mars) proudly show off historic explorers like Apollo 11's Eagle, the Lunar Roving Vehicles, Viking, Spirit, Opportunity and Ingenuity. The story then explains how these monuments differ from the dangerous debris crowding Earth's orbit. Built entirely in code and playable in any web browser, it helps young explorers learn space history and why we must keep space clean for future missions.

---

## 2. The challenge we chose

> *Since the 1960s, NASA has left hardware across the solar system: on the Moon, on Mars, and in deep space. Tell the story of some or all of this equipment in a way that introduces school-age space enthusiasts to the hardware and the science it made possible.*

**The problem we saw:** most children have never heard of Surveyor, ALSEP or Sojourner, and many mix up "hardware left in space" with "space junk." We wanted to tell this history in a way kids enjoy, and to clearly separate **historic artifacts** (respected monuments) from **orbital debris** (a real danger around Earth).

---

## 3. Our solution

An animated story with characters children can relate to, where real NASA hardware appears as **exhibits in a "space museum"**, each with a name tag and a short fact.

### The story in 8 scenes

| # | Scene | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A Big Dream** | Scientists dream of exploring the Moon and Mars and prepare a launch. |
| 2 | **Bye-Bye, Rocket Siblings!** | Spark launches; the rocket stages ("siblings") separate and stay behind in space. |
| 3 | **Space Is Getting Crowded!** | Spark meets old satellites and fast-moving debris in Earth orbit. |
| 4 | **Moon's Big Announcement** | Moony gives a museum tour of the hardware left on the Moon. |
| 5 | **Mars Gets Jealous** | Marsy shows off her own famous visitors, from Viking to Ingenuity. |
| 6 | **Too Much of a Good Thing** | Too many visitors cause a "traffic jam"; the narrator explains the debris problem. |
| 7 | **The Real Space Problem** | Deep-space explorers, then three real problems: collisions, danger to astronauts, risk to future missions. |
| 8 | **Let's Clean Up!** | How scientists track, design and remove debris; *"Keep space clean!"* |

## 4. How it works

- The whole episode is **drawn and animated in code**: characters, NASA hardware, backgrounds, camera moves and scene transitions.
- It runs from **one HTML file** hosted on **GitHub Pages**, with no installation, account or download needed.
- **Character voices:** each character has its own voice (browser text-to-speech; Microsoft Edge's natural voices sound best).
- **Subtitles** are shown for every line, which also helps viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing, or are learning English.
- **Music and sound effects** are generated by the code itself (no audio files).
- **Controls:** Play, Restart, Scene selector, Voices panel, toggles for subtitles, music and sound effects, full screen (`F`), and a button to record the episode as a video file.

### How to watch
1. Open **https://thegirlwhocoded.github.io/nokhotro/** (Microsoft Edge recommended).
2. Press **▶ Play** or the **Space bar**. Press **F** for full screen.

## 5. Benefits and intended impact

- **Teaches real history:** children learn the names, purposes and stories of more than 15 NASA missions.
- **Explains the science:** what each mission discovered or proved (soft landings, signs of past water on Mars, marsquakes, flight on Mars, exoplanets).
- **Clears up a misconception:** retired hardware on other worlds is history to respect; the dangerous debris is in **Earth's orbit**.
- **Builds responsibility:** viewers leave with a simple message, *take care of the space you explore*, and learn about real solutions (tracking, safe disposal, debris removal).
- **Easy to share:** works in any browser, so teachers can play it in a classroom.

---

## 6. How the project is creative

- Planets and spacecraft become **characters with personalities**: a proud "celebrity" Moon with a crown, a jealous Mars, cheering rocket-stage siblings.
- Real hardware is presented as a **space museum tour** with name tags, turning facts into a story.
- Humour (a "traffic jam" around planets, *"no parking without permission!"*) keeps young viewers engaged while delivering accurate science.

---

## 7. Factors we considered

- **Scientific accuracy:** facts checked against official NASA mission pages.
- **Age-appropriate** language, humour and pacing for roughly ages 9–14.
- A clear distinction between **historic artifacts** and **harmful debris**.
- **Accessibility:** subtitles, adjustable voices, and browser playback on any device.
- **Length:** about 7 minutes, to hold attention.

---

## 8. What we achieved and learned

- Completed a full 7-minute, 8-scene animated episode and published it online.
- Tested playback in different browsers and found Microsoft Edge gives the most natural voices.
- Learned how much hardware NASA has left across the solar system, and why historic artifacts and orbital debris are very different things.

### Future improvements
- Record human voice actors for every character.
- Add a short quiz at the end so classrooms can check what students learned.
- Add more hardware (for example the Voyager probes and Curiosity) and translate the episode into Bangla and other languages.
